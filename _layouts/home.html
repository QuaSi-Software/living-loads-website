Living Loads is a joint project that develops realistic demand profiles for buildings and districts by combining artificial intelligence with behavioral simulation. The project aims to create web-based software solutions that represent user-dependent energy demands - such as electricity, hot water, occupancy, and mobility - more accurately than conventional standard load profiles, thereby improving the planning and evaluation of energy systems.

The project addresses a central practical problem: standard load profiles insufficiently account for many relevant factors such as number of occupants, weather conditions, or socio-demographic characteristics, leading to poorly dimensioned energy systems. Living Loads solves this by developing AI models based on the open-source LoadProfileGenerator tool that convert complex usage patterns into practical parameters and enable comprehensive coupling with thermal building simulation and statistical aggregation to district load profiles.

The idea behind Living Loads originates with the work on the overarching software project QuaSi. During development of GenSim it became clear, that the commonly used load profiles for the presence of people in a building and their electricity and hot water consumption, need to be updated or, better yet, generated with realistic simulation tools.

The software, AI models and training data that will be produced as part of Living Loads will be published as open-source under the umbrella of QuaSi Software and there is a large overlap between the developers and organisations working on both projects. Concept

At the center of Living Loads sits an AI model which generates profiles based on input data about the building and its occupants. The inputs contain the key information on the building type, living areas, household size, weather data and the socio-demographic characteristics of the people living in the building. From these inputs the model generates time series data for the plug loads, hot water demand, electric vehicle charging demands as well as intermediary profiles for the presence and activity of the occupants. These outputs are optionally used in a follow-up thermal building simulation in order to also determine heating and cooling demands for the building.

The model is trained based on data generated with the open-source simulation tool LoadProfileGenerator (LPG), which simulates the behaviour of people in the context of energy-related activities at home. LPG has been in use for over a decade and can produce high quality, realistic load profiles, however also requires detailed input data and takes some time to calculate. The model is trained in such a way to reduce the necessary input data to the key aspects and speed up simulation.

Whole district profiles are aggregated from the results of individual residential units, taking the stochastic distribution of load peaks into account automatically, which further increases the quality of results as this is a real effect. For Living Loads the simulation model of LPG is also extended to include various options to model small and medium enterprises, which co-inhabit the buildings along residential units.

Partners

Three organisations are working on bringing Living Loads to life. The system analysis group ICE-2 of the Institute of Climate and Energy Systems of the Jülich Forschungszentrum are the developers of LoadProfileGenerator. Their work on the project includes updating LPG and implementing new features, extending the model scope to include small and medium enterprises, as well as applying their expertise with LPG where it is needed.

FACT, part of the TMM Group alongside Digital Building Industries AG, bring experience with creating AI models based on simulations to the project. Their work involves exploring the space of possible AI model architectures to find the best approach and building the training pipeline to create the models. They also work on developing apps based on the models to integrate into a larger ecosystem of related software.

Acting as project coordinator, siz energieplus works on many aspects of the project. This includes developing a model for the charging of electric vehicles, specifying and generating the training data, validating results using measurement data, coordinating the network of associated partners (see below) and developing other open-source software as part of the project.

A variety of organisations are supporting the joint partners working on Living Loads. These associated partners take on various roles ranging from beta-testing the developed software, lending their expertise, validating results to connecting research and education through the project and its results.

Funding
This project is funded in majority by the German Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Energy (BMWE), under grant number 03EN3135 in the research program "8. Energieforschungsprogramm". You can visit the SLP Ade project page on the official funding platform for more details.

The connected open source software project QuaSi was funded by the BMWE, under grant number 03EN3053 in the preceding "7. Energieforschungsprogramm".

Part of the current project is further development of the open source tool LoadProfileGenerator. You can find more details on its funding over the years on its homepage.
